# Glossary — every code and piece of project shorthand, in plain words

The project accumulated labels (H2, SCUM, C3, Q15…). **When writing for the author or anyone else, spell the idea out and leave the code out.** This file is the decoder. The authoritative, current status of each idea is `FINDINGS.md`; the original wording is `ANALYTIC_BRIEF.md` §5, which is older and in places superseded (see `STATUS.md`).

## The two shows

| Short | Full | One line |
|---|---|---|
| **KJB** | *Kill James Bond!* | UK trio (November Kelly, Abigail Thorn, Devon). Listener-funded via Patreon, no ads. Combative, ironic, in-group address. Started as a Bond show; season 4 is the "heist season". |
| **BC** | *The Bechdel Cast* | US duo (Caitlin Durante, Jamie Loftus) with a different guest every episode. Ad-supported. Teaching register: re-explains the Bechdel test from scratch every time. |

## The hypotheses (H1–H17), spelled out

Status is as of the last update to `FINDINGS.md`. "Held" means deliberately not pursued.

| Code | Plain-English name | What it says | Where it stands |
|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | **Style stays the same whatever the film** | Each show talks the same way on masculine-coded and feminine-coded films, and whatever the show's own politics. | Held up every time, down to ten-minute previews. |
| H2 | **BC hedges more than KJB** | BC uses more softeners ("I think", "kind of", "maybe"). Two causes found: BC addresses an imagined newcomer; and KJB hedges when it is building a new theory while BC speaks confidently when applying a known catalogue. | True as a tendency, not a law: the size varies and reversed on Atomic Blonde and Point Break. A hedge can also mean calibrated confidence, so counts need sorting by function (pending). |
| H3 | **Disgust vs anger** | BC condemns through disgust-type language ("gross", recoil); KJB through anger and contempt. | Demoted. The word-count version failed to replicate on Austin Powers. Survives only as a claim about what the language is doing, which needs hand-coding. |
| H4 | **Who gets undercut** | BC undercuts itself (apologies, "I'm bad at this"); KJB undercuts outward (the film, the industry). | Holds in the studio. A live audience pushes both shows toward the other's habits: the audience matters, a special occasion without one doesn't. |
| H5 | **Each show ironises what it is most earnest about** | KJB jokes about its own scoring system while applying it seriously; BC undercuts its own competence while showing it. | Supported on both sides. |
| H6 | **Where each show speaks from** | BC reads films from outside the harm, protectively; KJB from inside the position of the people harmed. | The strongest mirror in the corpus (same two transphobic scenes in Austin Powers, read from outside vs inside). Also shows KJB's reading flips from prosecution to longing on feminine-coded films. |
| H7 | **Systems vs relationships** | KJB reads films as systems, objects and institutions (watches, money, intelligence services); BC tracks representation and relationships beat by beat. | Supported; the cleanest example is Atomic Blonde. |
| H8 | **Theory vs catalogue, and how each treats its own scoring tool** | KJB builds frameworks and ironises its rubric; BC applies a fixed test with developing case law and takes it seriously. Verdicts differ because the *instruments* weigh different things. | Confirmed; the only real verdict split (Atomic Blonde) reduces to instrument design. |
| H9 | **Same conclusions** | Behind the different styles the two shows reach the same feminist conclusions about the same films. **The load-bearing claim of the book.** | The standing challenge ("find one film where the conclusions genuinely differ") is unmet across eight full-length pairs. |
| H10 | **Who gets the awards** | BC gives its scores as gifts to admired women; KJB gives rosettes to villains and discarded characters. | Supported, widened to "disposable men and marginal characters". |
| H11 | **How queer readings are made** | BC defers from outside ("we're straight ladies, queer listeners help us") and imports the inside view through guests; KJB asserts from inside as its default voice. | Refined to "constitutive vs imported inside-ness". |
| H12 | **Citation habits** | Originally "BC cites, KJB doesn't". | **Corrected**: KJB cites too (Carole Pateman, by name); the difference is a source you defer to vs doctrine you absorb. |
| H13 | **Self-disclosure** | BC's personal stories are rapport; KJB's do analytic work and are triggered by the film. | Supported, but how *often* hosts say "I" is unreliable; look at what the personal talk does. |
| H14 | **Do KJB's in-jokes harden over time?** | The prediction that KJB's private vocabulary would pile up and turn into a gate that holds the audience in. | **Tested and failed.** Density is flat across four-plus years. The audience is held together by shared position (a trans community), not by lore. |
| H15 | **Held — not pursued** | A hypothesis about the hosts' personal history. | Out of scope. Do not investigate, assert or reason about it, in essays or in working notes. |
| H16 | **Different tools of disapproval** | Both shows are egalitarian-left but enforce differently: BC through disgust/purity-type language, KJB through approach-style aggression. Tools of disapproval can be separated from the politics they serve. | Supported, and tested outside the left (Atomic Blonde reviewers): disgust-based condemnation appeared only in a religious content-warning review. Generalised: KJB handles status by occupying it ironically, BC by dissolving it. |
| H17a | **A film does different work on different viewers** | Both shows say the same film is a target for critique for some viewers and an aspiration for others who miss the point. | Supported; both shows agree on this model. |
| H17b | **Each show's style sorts its audience** | A flattening, hedging register reads as safe to some listeners and weak to others. | Supported in outline. The author's own "BC feels understimulating" reaction is an example. |
| H17c | **KJB builds new frameworks, BC applies known ones** | The "altitude" claim. | Real and survives the live-vs-studio check, but cannot be pinned on temperament because funding model and teaching mission explain it too. Held as "two different kinds of sophistication": KJB generates theory, BC questions its own measuring tool. |

## Shorthand inside the shows

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Bechdel test** | Two named women talk to each other about something other than a man. BC re-explains it every episode. |
| **Nipple scale** | BC's 0–5 rating, scored by each person, often "dedicated" to admired women. |
| **SCUM** | KJB's scoring system. Four axes: **S**marm, **C**ultural insensitivity, **U**nprovoked violence, **M**isogyny. Scored by consensus and compared to a database of earlier films ("same as Charlie's Angels: Full Throttle"). |
| **Smarm** | Self-satisfied, congratulatory style. It is the axis that explains why SCUM scores Atomic Blonde so low while the nipple scale scores it well. |
| **Kronsteen Rosette** | Award to a villain or henchperson who "goes above and beyond". Named for the chess master in *From Russia with Love*. |
| **Kaufman Star** | Award tied to the hosts' horniness toward the screen. First appears around episode 24. |
| **Goodnight Cross** | Award for an underappreciated good character (e.g. a film's gay-coded hairdresser in The Rock). |
| **GIRTH** | A four-axis scoring system invented on-mic in the Rock episode and never used again. The project's example of a bit that didn't survive. |
| **Waingrow** | A unit taken from KJB's *Heat* episode: the uncontrollable violent element that wrecks a heist, used as a measurable property of other films. |
| **Heist season** | KJB's season 4: Ocean's, Fast & Furious, Point Break and the rest. |
| **Bonus / ".5" episode** | Patreon-only episode. KJB gives the free public a ~10-minute preview. |

## Method words

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Matched pair** | A film both shows covered. 13 films: 8 where both are full episodes, 5 where KJB exists only as a preview. |
| **Preview pair** | One of the five. Compare KJB's preview only with BC's first ten minutes. |
| **Register** | A show's characteristic way of talking: swearing, hedging, how it addresses listeners. |
| **Hedge** | A softener ("maybe", "I think", "kind of"). |
| **Word list / lexicon** | A fixed list of words counted as hedges, swearing, apologies, "I", disgust or anger words. Lives in `tagging/lexicons.json`. |
| **Per 1k** | Rate per 1,000 words. Always read beside the raw count. |
| **ASR** | Automatic speech recognition: the transcripts are machine captions, with mangled names and no speaker labels. |
| **Function, not lexical shadow** | The unit of analysis is what an utterance *does*, not the word that happens to appear. Learned the hard way twice. |
| **Confound** | A second variable that moves with the one you care about. Format, nationality, funding, gender and era all line up between the two shows, so no single cause can be isolated. |
| **Pre-registration** | Writing the prediction down before reading the evidence (done for the Sarkeesian episode and the conservative-reviewer test). |
| **Schröder lens** | Arnold Schröder's *Fight Like an Animal* material, used only to describe styles of disapproval and status. Not a diagnosis of anyone. |
| **Third pole** | The test using conservative reviewers on Atomic Blonde, to see whether the findings survive outside the two shows' shared politics. |
| **Looping effect** | Ian Hacking's idea that a label changes the people it's applied to, who then change the label. Appears in the Christianity on the Spectrum material. |
